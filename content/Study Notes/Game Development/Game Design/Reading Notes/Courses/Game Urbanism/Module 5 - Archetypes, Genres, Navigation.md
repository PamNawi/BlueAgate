# City Archetypes
https://www.numberanalytics.com/blog/urban-archetypes-guide
An urban archetypes refer to repeating patterns and forms that appears on urban environments. They can refer to societal behaviors, prosperity, functions, locations, transportation modes, architecture and even neighborhoods, technologies, preconceptions on public space and specific elements. They are important because they embody the collective memory and identity of a city, shaping the way their citizens interact with the environment and each other. Urban can be seen as a manifestation of a city's history, culture and values, making them an essential aspect of urban planning and design.

Many of the European metropolis maintain their old medieval core, roads and rail-based networks,  industrial areas, dense & low-density suburbs and sub-centers. Some elements of the architecture also are presented on the archetypal features like the widow's walk, a railed rooftop platform often having a cupola where frequently found on 19-century North America coastal houses. The name is said to come from the wives of mariners, who would watch their spouses return often in vain as the ocean took their lives. Beyond their use as viewing platforms, they are frequently built around the chimney of the residence potentially creating access to the structure.

![[Pasted image 20260411201019.png]]

Other common structure found in coastal cities on harbor districts are the lighthouses that served as a beacon for navigational aid marking dangerous coastlines, hazardous shoals, reefs, rocks and safe entries to harbors, they also assist in aerial navigation. They where widely used, the number of operational lighthouses has declined due to the expense maintenance and the advent of much cheaper, more sophisticated and effective electronic navigational systems.

The settlements of the Greek Islands are defined by a large number of churches, relatively small single- or two family homes, with simple architectural lines, and a strong presence of whites and blues. They are oriented towards the sea. 

![[Pasted image 20260429114416.png]]

On 1938 there was a outbreak of cholera on Greece and a national order mandated that all houses should be painted with a whitewash that contained limestone to control the outbreak. It was a very effective way to sanitize the homes and control the disease. The fishermen painted their windows and shutters with whatever paint was left after painting their boats and normally the cheapest paint available was blue.  The blue paint was made using a mixture of limestone and a cleaning product called "loulaki" which was a kind of blue talcum powder. The military dictatorship that took over Greece in 1967 made the blue and white coloring of Greek island houses mandatory, they believe that would inspire patriotism and were the reflective of the Greek nationalism associated with the country flag. The law was passed on 1974 to mandate the painting of the Greek island homes in blue and white. [Source](https://greeklearninghub.com/7620-2/)
