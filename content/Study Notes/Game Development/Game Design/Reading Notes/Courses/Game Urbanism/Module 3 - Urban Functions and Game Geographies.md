urban functions are a core concept of urbanism
Urban functions are the reasons cities exist; what cities do and are meant to do. Cities are built to facilitate and support them, and in turn evolve as those functions themselves evolve.

Video games often ignore the concept of urban functions, and this occasionally leads to aesthetically disappointing, paper-thin, or incomplete settlements

Additionally when considering urban functions cities can be fleshed out in more interesting ways. They became even more fertile.


Contemporary urban functions:
residence, commerce, production, consumption, human, reproduction, security, communication, education, transportation, culture, ideology, the circulation of capital, administration, socialization and access to drinking water and energy

Medieval settlement:
defense and religion. The need for water would be satisfied through wells and nearby lakes, and not through a network of running water pipes or bottled water.

Deep space settlement
Shielding from radiation and artifical gravity could also be functions

Functions such as communication and transportation are also important that they determine they physical limits of urbanism

Urban functions can help designers creatively and easily differentiate between settlements of their worlds

An open world map does not need to be populated by similar-but-reskinned towns and cities. An exploration outpost, a heavily industrialized city, a fortified outpost, a capital focused on administration, a mining village and a specialized transportation hub, can all help bring variety and imply a complex geographical location

![[Pasted image 20260318131241.png]]

To balance fiction and mechanics, and to support the suspension of disbelief, it helps if the game functions of spaces are reminiscent of functions of represented spaces. Changes and variations are obviously allowed.

Aligning game & real-life functions makes game cities more convincing, more legible, and thus easier to actually use.

Functions and Urban Land Uses
Urban functions take up actual space as land uses.

The space that cities reserve and assign to specific activities and functions is what urban planners and geographers commonly describe as "land use". Land use planning is the spatial organization of said uses.

We need to know where our virtual people live, and where they work. Where they shop, where their parks and places of assembly are they entertain themselves, where they bury their dead, where they travel to and from
