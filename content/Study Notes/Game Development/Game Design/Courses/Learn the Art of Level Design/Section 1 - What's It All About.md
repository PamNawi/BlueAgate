# What is a Level Designer?
Its a person who the primary role will be to design challenging, engaging and fun levels that can fit into the structure and pace of a game.

- Use a game engine and 3D modeling applications to create white-box levels
- Work to design brief/ concept art/ script
- Implement level flow, structure and pacing using the game mechanics
- Design and integrate puzzles / missions
- Weave narration and artistic themes within the level and gameplay mechanics
- Implement enemy / character / loot / game objects in an engaging and challenging way
- Refine your level from high-level concept to final product using player feedback
- Play and balance your level

### What does one not do?
- Crete environment art
- Create animation
- Programmer

⭐ _"Level design is an important, creative, challenging and fundamental aspect of game development"_

# What is a Level?
In context of games a level can be one of two things:
- On RPGs its an accumulator of experience points
- Its a physical location, designed with objectives and progression

⭐ _"A section of a game that must be defeated to move forward or progress, and resides within much larger whole"_

# What are Constraints?
- Constraints can be external (beyond your control):
	- Time
	- Budget
	- Hardware
	- Team Size
	- Management / Requirements
- They can also be design constraints:
	- The main objective of your level (fixed path, follow a character...)
	- Pre-determined design
	- The number of levels in your game (scope)
	- Number of players

⭐ _"They matter because they will determine your scope ... Scope will determine quality. Quality must be kept up, and a lot of decisions will be made based on your principal constraint."
- Be realistic
- Use the mechanics
- Look at other levels and borrow from them
- Avoid feature creep
- Take your time
- Don't make your programmers cry (much)
- Don't be afraid of the delete key
- Make it a group decision

# Level Components
Breaking down the level into components, helps you organize your thoughts and allows you to create a "to do" list. On general the components inside a level are:
- Physical structures
- Borders, boundaries and beats (3 beats):
	- _Borders:_ The end and start of something new like entering or leaving a new zone / room
	- _Boundaries:_ Define the edge of your world / level
	- _Beats:_ A little like borders, but these can be narrative and story elements too
- Positive and Negative space
- Dynamic Interactive content
- Landmarks
- Points of Interest
- Visual Guidance
- Framing / Composition
- Clear min and sub-objectives
- Compelling gameplay that keeps the player glued

Things a level doesn't need:
- No landmarks or visual language ( they will get loss)
- Symmetry - Its terrific (Maybe a good way of create unsettling ambiences?)
- Too many paths and dead ends (prune your paths, the challenge should be on mechanics)
- Déjà vu / Repetition - Add some variety on your environment
- Not meeting expectations
- Lack of any pace - too slow or even too quick
