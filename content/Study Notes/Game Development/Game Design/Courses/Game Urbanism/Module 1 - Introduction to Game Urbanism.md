Our Goal is to understand how urban environments work and how they are constructed.

To find out what cities are and wat they look like, and adjust them to the needs of specific games and worlds.


# What are game cities?
All cities and all urban environments found in games
- Hopefully believable illusions
- Game spaces to be approached via level and game design
- Simulations (up to a point at least).
- Narrative actors, and diegetic scenery
- Pithy showcases of world-building
- Incredibly varied
- Stages for gameplay and storytelling
- Complex configurations that need to be made legible

# What does Game Urbanism do?
Design the urban structure, environments and system of games.
- Steals from real-life urbanism, engineering, and architecture
- Applies level and game design to urban environments
- Simulates civic lifes and societies
- Abstracts heavily
- Engages in world building and crafts histories
- Creates civics concepts


Game urbanism combines level design, game design, narrative design and world building.
![[Pasted image 20260225131722.png]]

Building good cities for video games is mainly about doing 3 things right:
- World Building : The world must be believable
- Level Design : We need to be sure that the space is playable
- Smoke & Mirrors : We are always doing a simulation and selling smoking & mirrors of a simulation

## The absolute basics of city crafting:
- Every city has to make sense: Realism leads to believability and believability can lead to spatial immersion.
- Do not think exclusively on the architectural scale. Think like urban planners and geographers. Let your city tells its story, and inspire your game, world, narrative and level design.
- Research your urbanism! You will find solutions you never knew you needed. Remember that all urban centres are historical societal constructs; thus dynamic perpetual works in progress.
- Focus on urban functions; they are reasons cities exist - what cities do and are meant to do. Cities are built to facilitate and support them.
- All urban space has been planned.
- Approach the city as whole. Think from general to specific (Or don't.)
- Cities are internally differentiated (They are built on districts -> This connects with the cgma course)
- Urban infrastructure is the city's skeleton
- All settlements have form and structure
- Consider arrivals/entrances/landmarks/boundaries of your cities.
- Urban lifes matters! (NPCs needs to feel alive)

Cities are never an assortment of scattered buildings. They need structure at all levels.

# The 11 Questions to defining a game city

- Where is the city?
	- Every city has to be somewhere. The location of the city determines much of its initial space and available space. Locations provide with limits.
	- This location is never randomly chosen, and provides the place with its foundational geography and topography
	- An urban center can be by the sea, in the mountains, split by a river, somewhere in the arctic, in space, on a plain, even under the sea, as long as life can be plausibly sustained.
	- Interesting examples: Bogota, Paris, Erfoud, Malé, Tenochtitlan, Hydra
	![[Pasted image 20260225133313.png]]
	- Interesting examples in games: Rapture (Bioshock), Citadel (Mass effect), Oakmont (), Providence Oaks (Lakes), Baghdad (Assassins Creed), Gerudo Town (The Legend of Zelda : Breath of the Wild).

- When is the city?
	- Time is important as it determines the exact point in a city's history, the available technology, and even aspects such as predominant vehicles or fashions
	- The lens of time answers questions such as: Is this a time of war? Is the city ancient or brand new? What was the latest major event?
![[Pasted image 20260304135608.png]]
![[Pasted image 20260304135738.png]]
	- Things remain, we don't demolish things, we try to keep/maintain our buildings

- Why was the city founded?
	- The founding of the city is an important historical event, and is not a decision to be taken lightly. Deciding to build a town at this exact spot always happens for a reason.
	- Political, religious, financial, cultural and other societal important reasons, can lead to the founding of a city.
	- Agricultural centre, trade hub, holy city, fortress town, major harbour, industrial town, tourist town, logistics centre, etc.
![[Pasted image 20260304140535.png]]
	- Imagine if they heard about... BRASILIA.
![[Pasted image 20260304140837.png]]

- How Big is the city?
	- From a tiny village to a planet-spanning megalopolis, settlements come in all sizes
	- A difference in size is not merely quantitative difference. Its also qualitative
	- As cities grow in size (A process that also implies passage of time) they grow richer in functions and complexity
	- A villager interacts with people they know. In a metropolis you mainly interact with strangers
![[Pasted image 20260304142539.png]]
- Where do people live and work?
	- Two of the most important functions of any settlements are residence and work
	- Where and who people live and work explains much about the political economy of the society they are in
![[Pasted image 20260304143017.png]]
![[Pasted image 20260304143259.png]]

- How do people and things move around?
	- Transportation is a key function of the city, and one that determines many of its metrics
	- The moving of humans and things can use the same means. Or not.
	- Technology, the organization of society, climate, topography, and culture all play a defining role in transportation
	- Commuting is something to consider, as are countless number of other types of movement
![[Pasted image 20260304143824.png]]
![[Pasted image 20260304144009.png]]
- What is the city's structure?
	- Structure is the logic with which cities arrange their functions in space
	- Structure determines form, and arises from the needs and planning of an urban centre
	- Structure answers the question: "How is city space organised?" - "Where is everything?"
![[Pasted image 20260304144214.png]]
![[Pasted image 20260304144229.png]]

- What's the architecture like?
	- Architecture shapes the individual buildings within each settlement
	- Architecture players a huge role in the creation of the image of each city
	- Architectural styles and rhythms are determined by factors such as ideology, politics, modes of production, technology, materials, popular entertainment, belief, systems, climate, and even the predominant type of family.
![[Pasted image 20260304144751.png]]

- Can you describe the society and city's theme?
	- The polis always referred to the citizens and not the built environment
	- Its is societies that construct and guide the evolution of cities, even if pre-existing civic elements influence societies too
	- What kind of people live in your city? How are they governed? What do they want?
	- What is your city about? How does it showcase its core theme(s)?
![[Pasted image 20260304145430.png]]
![[Pasted image 20260304145558.png]]
- What stories does this city tell?
	- The are stories of the city, and then there are sotories in the city
	- Every city comes with a history and every city does environmental storytelling on the grand scale. Wether its wants to or not.
	- Cities are packed with humans are often important symbols themselves, and offer complexity, contrasts and infinite diversity. They are excellent, fertille storytelling stages.
![[Pasted image 20260304145756.png]]	![[Pasted image 20260304145857.png]]

- What Game is this city for?
	- Is it an open world game? Does it consist of discreet levels? Is there a map available?
	- Is it a 3D or a 2D game? Is the game city experienced in first or third person? What type of camera is employed?
	- Is it for a narrative-heavy game? A stealth game? An action adventure? Or is the city meant to host an adventure game. A roleplaying game? What are the game's core mechanis and how is traversal handled?

- What makes your city memorable?
	- What are the Points of Interest on those maps?

# Inspiration and Concepts
- Inspiration specially when it comes to game cities, is fuelled by research and a richness of references
- Real life and historical references are absolutely crucial. References from art and literature are also important
- Reserarch compotemporary, historical, imaginary, speculative, utopian and dystopian urbanism (and architecture) across all mediums
- Inspiration does not have to exclusively come from urban and architectural thought. REad, play, listen and watch wide
- Possible starting points include: unique answers to any of the 11 questions, natural geography, technology, special materials, history, age, specific, restrictions, ideology, politics, geopolitics, core functions and more.
- A complete game city concept also covers the place's aesthetics, and takes genre into account. In Grim Fandango, for example, Aztec influences and modernism work well with each other, and determine the architecture, the visual style, and the setting of the game.

# The Three Approaches on City Building
- Choose an existing or historical city, and abstract it to a manageable level. Research. Identify defining characteristics and symbols. Apply generalization / simplification (Example: Any Assassin's creed game)
- Heavily modify and/or combine existing or historical cities; mix their elements, and disguise them. (Example: Mafia III and Terry Pratchett's Ankh-Morpork)
- Create a city from scratch drawing from a variety of inspirations. Start by answering the 11 questions. Make it interesting and believable. (Examples: Midgar, Rubacava, City 17, Rapture)
